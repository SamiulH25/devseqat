# Roven: World Lore Bible

The setting reference for the continent of **Roven**. This document sets the names, history, faiths, magic, peoples, and monsters of the world.

> **Status:** Drafted as the canonical lore direction (2026-11-22). In-game data files (`Data/world.json`, `Data/factions.json`, `Data/orders.json`, `Data/troops.json`, `Data/events.json`) still use the older placeholder names until a dedicated rename pass migrates them (see the mapping table at the end).

## 1. The Land: Roven

Roven is a single landmass broken through its middle by a deep saltwater basin called **the Maren** *(southerners and westerners call it the Maren; northern sailors call it the Midsea)*. Two bodies of water — the **Bay of Mists** in the west and the **Salt Sea** in the east — bite into the flanks of the continent, while a high chain of mountains, **the Spine**, cuts across the interior.

Only one dry neck of highland connects the northern kingdoms to the southern sands: **the Tarn Pass**, a narrow fortified isthmus above the Maren. Whoever holds the Pass controls the overland road between north and south.

The country changes sharply from one edge to the other:
- **The North (Skar):** Cold fjords, pine taiga, tundra, and Storm Isle off the Frost Channel. Short summers, thin soil, good timber and herring.
- **The West (Brevon & Brinemere):** Wet farmland, abbey meadows, oak woods, and — where the western rivers meet the Maren — tidal salt-marshes and island lagoons.
- **The Centre (Vespra & Tarn):** Rolling hills, walled valleys, alpine crags along the Spine, and the grey highland passes.
- **The East (Naryn):** Open grass steppe running out to the brackish ports of the Salt Sea.
- **The South (Qadir):** Rain-shadow badlands, red desert, snowmelt oases, and spice-rich coasts along the Maren and Crescent Gulf.

## 2. History

Roven's history is not one clean story. The continent has never been united under a single crown, and most of its borders were drawn by debts, bad wills, failed harvests, and border wars rather than grand prophecies.

### The Older World *(beyond living memory)*
Long before any of the present realms took shape, older peoples lived along the Maren and in the high valleys of the Spine when the **Tide** ran thicker in the earth. They cut the first mountain roads, raised the **standing stones**, and fought forgotten wars against things that are mostly gone or driven into the crags.

Almost none of their names survive. What remains are **Wells** where the earth still hums, weathered stone rings that farmers plough around, sunken foundations beneath newer towns, and a handful of heirloom blades and saints' relics whose makers nobody remembers. People across Roven share a quiet, unspoken sense that the world used to be taller and stranger than it is now.

### How the Seven Realms Formed
Each realm grew out of its own mess:

- **Brevon** was four quarrelling river-duchies and a stubborn coast (the Bayfolk) hammered into one kingdom two centuries ago by a ruthless marriage alliance and two long sieges. The great houses still remember when they were kings in their own right, and they guard the line between noble blood and common levies fiercely.
- **Brinemere** was born from a defaulted loan, not a heroic rebellion. Seventy years ago, a Brevon king mortgaged the south-western lagoon ports to the island banking houses to fund a winter campaign in Skar. When he died without a clear heir, the guilds locked the causeways, hired pike companies, and dared Brevon to march through chest-deep marsh to collect. Bayfolk still live under Brinemere's banner along the contested coast.
- **Vespra** is older than Brevon: a jealous federation of walled patrician cities that swore a mutual defence **Compact** a hundred and fifty years ago after eastern horse-riders burned two of their towns. In peacetime the cities scheme against each other; the alpine **Cragfolk** only honour the Compact when the silver arrives on time.
- **Qadir** across the Maren is the oldest unbroken civilization on Roven — oasis dynasties that kept libraries, glassworks, and star-charts while western lords still lived behind timber palisades. Internally it is divided: the rich **Qadiri** oasis lords look down on the nomadic **Rimal** who guide (and raid) their caravans, and the **Marenites** along the coast answer to whoever pays best.
- **Skar** has never kept one king for longer than a single lifetime. A generation ago a fjord-lord united the clans long enough to raid down the Bay of Mists. When he died, his sons split the fleet, and the eldest is now burning Brevon's northern shore to prove he deserves his father's hall.
- **Naryn** was a dozen feuding steppe banners until **Temir Naryn** broke the other khans ninety years ago and rode west to the Salt Sea. Rather than raze the coastal ports, his grandson married a port-heiress — creating the mail-clad **Saltmen**, whom the pure steppe **Free Banners** half-despise as soft.
- **Tarn** began as a fortified hospice and chapel left to a brotherhood of warrior-monks by a guilty Vespran duke on his deathbed. Over two centuries of kings dumping inconvenient second sons, disgraced knights, and pious grants into it, the **Chapter of Tarn** quietly grew into a state of its own — holding the Tarn Pass and riding into the highlands whenever old barrows wake.

### The Hollow Crown *(~40 years ago)*
Within living memory, a celebrated knight-commander who hit his Aura Wall — and could not bear growing old and ordinary — went into the oldest highland barrows looking for a way past it. What he brought back was the **Black Flame**. It broke his wall and let him cut down men like wheat, but it ate his mind from the inside. He crowned himself with a blackened iron circlet and burned three baronies before his own sworn brothers hunted him down in the snow. They burned his body, but his followers scattered rather than died, and the **Hollow Crown** still finds ambitious or cornered knights in the dark.

### The Five-Year War *(12 years ago — the recent wound)*
Twelve years ago, Brevon and Vespra bled each other white over the central marches and the Bayfolk trade tolls. Neither side won; both emptied their treasuries and left tens of thousands in shallow graves.

That war explains the world on Day 1:
- Roven is crawling with disbanded veterans, landless second sons, and **free companies** looking for pay.
- **Brevon and Vespra** sit on a bitter, brittle truce while their neighbours smell blood: **Skar** is raiding Brevon's coast, and **Naryn** has crossed into Vespra's eastern frontier over a caravan dispute.
- With garrisons thinned and fresh battlefields unburied, **the Unkept** — Kobs, Stonebacks, and the Barrow-Dead — are creeping back out of the woods and hills.

## 3. Faiths, Magic & Aura

Magic in Roven is real, dangerous, and rare enough that ordinary soldiers still cross themselves when they see it. There is no single pantheon; four distinct religious traditions overlap, argue, and look down on one another.

### The Four Faiths

1. **The Vigil** *(Brevon, Vespra, Brinemere, Tarn)*
   Reveres **the Grey Light** — the first cold light of dawn that pushes back the night and keeps the dead in the earth — and the **Martyrs and Saints**, real men and women of past centuries whose bones, shrines, and vows still hold power.
   - **Brevon** keeps wealthy abbeys and cathedral schools; its **Lectors and Magisters** treat Faith as law and high learning.
   - **Tarn** is the austere, militant wing: grey cloaks, shaved heads, no gold, no chains, no Craft. They think Brevon's abbots are fat and Vespra's patricians are half-pagan.
   - **Vespra** kneels in chapel on feast days and quietly hires **hedge-witches and Crimson Crones** out of the hill-caves when a war turns ugly.
   - **Brinemere** treats priests like marine insurance: pay for a blessing before the galley sails, then let the **Guild Alchemists** keep mixing fire-oil in the Arsenal.

2. **The High Sun** *(Qadir)*
   Across the Maren, Qadir reveres the **Unblinking Sun** — strict, scholarly, and mathematical. To Qadiri **Star-Readers and Astrologers**, reading the heavens and bending heat-haze is not dirty Craft; it is reading the Creator's ledger. *(Tarn calls it sorcery anyway. Qadir calls Tarn superstitious hill-monks.)*

3. **The Old Names** *(Skar)*
   No church and no scripture — only blood-oaths, carved timbers, and the **Old Names** of sea, pine, frost, and beast. **Rune-singers and Skalds** sing into the wind and leave salt on the standing stones. In the deep taiga, **bear-cults** believe a warrior can borrow a beast's temper in battle.

4. **The Wide Sky** *(Naryn)*
   The steppe clans swear by the **Open Sky** and hold **wolves sacred** as the Sky's hounds — killing wolves angers the clans. Their **Drummers and Sky-Callers** do not see themselves as sorcerers; they are calling down the weather that belongs to the grass.

### The Tide, Wells & Omen
- **The Tide:** Casters do not speak of mana. They speak of **the Tide**, which rises and ebbs like the sea — pooling in **Wells** (shrines, healing springs, standing stones, ruins, and bloody battlefields). Faith runs stronger by day; Craft runs stronger at night. When two circles fight on the same ground, they drink from the same well; pushing past it makes the Tide bite back.
- **Faith vs. Craft:** Common folk say **Faith asks** (through prayer, vows, and saints' relics), while **Craft takes** (pulling on storms, fevers, stars, or blood without permission). Working Craft too hard raises **Omen**: crows trail the baggage train, men wake with fever, villages bar their gates, and nearby barrows stir.
- **Alchemy:** Brinemere's Guild Alchemists insist their fire-pots and smoke are chemistry, not witchery, though priests and villagers rarely see the difference.
- **Necromancy:** Universally hated by every people on Roven. Even Vespra's war-witches will not touch it. It is the rot left behind when too many men die unburied where the Tide runs thick.

### Aura & The Black Flame
Ordinary people do not view **Aura** as magic — they see it as **will hammered into flesh**. Perhaps one fighting man in several thousand ever wakes it. An **Aura Master** is a once-in-a-generation figure whose name goes into songs; soldiers who see a lit blade cut through a shield wall talk about it for the rest of their lives.

Priests claim Aura is the favour of the saints; Skalds call it old hero-blood waking; Qadiri sages call it a spark of the sun. Nobody truly knows. What every warrior knows is **the Wall** — the limit most men never pass, broken only through a duel to the death, a lonely vigil, or holding a line against impossible odds.

The **Black Flame** is hated because it is a shortcut: it burns straight through the Wall without a trial, feeding on something older and hungrier than human will, until nothing is left inside the armour.

### Houses, Dukedoms & How Each Realm Is Ruled

Nobility does not look the same across Roven. A castle esquire in Brevon and a steppe Noyan in Naryn would barely recognize each other's claim to rank.

- **Brevon — Crown, Dukedoms & Blood:**
  A feudal monarchy built out of four older river-duchies and the Bayfolk coast. The **King** sits by inheritance, checked by **Great Dukes** who still remember when their grandfathers wore crowns of their own, with Counts and Barons sworn beneath them:
  - **The Royal Demesne (Crownlanders):** Rich abbey valleys and tournament towns around the river crossings. The Crown relies on **House Vauclair**'s old alliances and the church's **Lectors** to keep the great dukes in line.
  - **The Northern Coast (Dukedom of Dammartin):** Salt-stained harbour lords bearing the brunt of Skar's longship raids. They resent the inland dukes for sending promises instead of ships.
  - **The Southern Reach (Dukedom of Peyrolles):** Border-bay houses who still keep the deeds to the lagoon ports lost to Brinemere seventy years ago — and push for war every time the treasury recovers.
  - **The Eastern March (March of Coralto):** Hard, scarred **Marcher** houses who fought the Five-Year War against Vespra and still hold grudges over burned manors and unransomed kin.
  - *Noble vs. Common:* Brevon guards the line between noble blood and common levies harder than anywhere else. Common soldiers can only cross it by being knighted on the field for deeds.

- **Vespra — Signorial Houses & Crag-Margraves:**
  No king. Vespra is governed by a **Council of Signors** drawn from the patrician cities (*Solstice, Ursel, Radiance*). Power rests with great **Signorial Houses** — dynasties of silk-merchants, armourers, and bankers who rule walled towns like private estates and settle rivalries with duelists from the Red Rose or hired sellswords. Along the northern Spine, hereditary **Crag-Lords (Margraves)** hold the slate-roofed pass-keeps; they despise city politics and only answer the Compact's summons when the silver is weighed in full.

- **Skar — Jarls, Sea-Kings & Holtmen:**
  No permanent crown and no written feudal ladder. Power belongs to **Jarls** who hold fjord timber-halls and longship fleets, bound together loosely by a seasonal **Ting** (assembly). A **High Jarl** rules only while his raids bring back silver and grain; when he grows old or unlucky, rival jarls walk away. Inland, the **Holtmen** of the deep taiga answer to hearth-chiefs and hunt-elders rather than coastal jarls, and blood-feuds pass down generations until paid in blood-price or steel.

- **Qadir — Emirates, Oasis Dynasties & Desert Sheikhs:**
  Ruled by a **Grand Emir** from the wealthiest snowmelt oasis, with great **Emirs** (the settled **Qadiri**) holding walled garden-valleys as hereditary fiefs. Their courts run on patronage, marriage ties, and household mamluks loyal only to the Emir who armed them. Between the oases, **Rimal** sheikhs hold no castles — only wells, salt-tracks, and caravan rights — while the **Marenite** corsair beys along the coast pay tribute when the Grand Emir's galleys are near and raid freely when they are not.

- **Naryn — The Khagan, Noyans & Salt-Beys:**
  The **Khagan** of the **Naryn bloodline** (descended from Temir Naryn) rules by personal prestige and the loyalty of the **Noyans**, lords of the great *Tumens* (Free Banners). There are no landed dukedoms on the open grass — a Noyan's wealth is his herds, his riders, and his share of plunder. Along the Salt Sea, however, the **Salt-Beys** of the **Saltmen** (descended from the Khagan's marriage into the old coastal families) hold walled harbour towns and pay tribute in iron and salt, caught between steppe contempt and coastal riches.

- **Brinemere — The Doge, Counting Houses & Fen-Reeves:**
  A merchant republic with no dukes and no hereditary titles. A **Doge** is elected for life by the **Great Counting** — the heads of the richest **Brinemen** banking and shipping houses — and watched closely by the **Guild Syndics** and **Arsenal Masters**. Castles and towns are chartered, leased, or mortgaged rather than inherited. Out in the tidal reeds, the **Fenmen** answer to rough local **Reeves** who run barges, smugglers' channels, and marsh-levies, and ignore the Doge until the salt-tax goes up.

- **Tarn — The Chapter & Pass-Wardens:**
  No noble houses, no dukedoms, no heirs. Tarn is ruled by a **Grand Master** elected by the **Chapter Council** of Knight-Commanders. Men who take the grey cloak surrender their family names and inheritance — which is why Brevon and Vespra dukes quietly send troublesome second sons and disgraced cousins to Tarn, and why the Chapter never fractures in succession wars. Beneath the sworn brothers sit the **Pass-Wardens**, hereditary highland bailiffs who keep the canyon gates, tolls, and villages in the Chapter's name.

## 5. The Unkept *(Monsters & The Dead)*

There is no dark empire of monsters. **The Unkept** are simply the things the older world never finished clearing out — pushed back into the deep taiga, high crags, and barrows when realms are strong, and creeping back onto the roads whenever human kings bleed each other white. Soldiers say a province is *"going unkept"* when the garrisons leave and the woods start taking the roads back.

| Roven Name | Replaces | What They Are |
|---|---|---|
| **Kobs & Hobs** *(leaders: Hob-Kings / Taskers)* | Goblins, Hobgoblins, Goblin Boss | Scavenger tunnel-folk who breed in abandoned mines, ruined watchtowers, and hill-caves. Mean, starving, and cowardly until they outnumber you three to one. The larger iron-toothed fighters are **Hobs**; big warrens break hill-wolves for **Warg-Kobs**. |
| **Stonebacks** | Trolls | Huge highland and deep-forest brutes. Peasants believe they are half-stone, half-moss — sunlight stiffens their hide and slows them to a crawl, while their torn flesh knits shut in the dark. |
| **Ettins** | Ogres | Not true monsters, but a dying, dim-witted kindred of giant-men from the high passes. They care nothing for kings or faiths, only salt beef, iron, and silver — which is why a mercenary captain can occasionally hire them in a tavern. |
| **Crag-Wyrms** | Wyverns | True dragons are gone (if they ever existed). Crag-Wyrms are their last mean, leathery, venomous cousins, nesting on highland cliffs and desert mesas. |
| **Weavers** *(apex: Brood-Widows)* | Giant Spiders | Deep-forest ambushers that web whole logging roads shut and starve nearby villages if left unchecked. |
| **Grim-Wolves** | Dire Wolves | Huge pack-leaders of the northern and steppe woods. Naryn clans hold wolves sacred as the Sky's hounds. |
| **Crag-Bears** | Cave Bears / Great Bears | Massive highland and taiga bears, revered by Skar's woodland bear-cults. |
| **The Unquiet** *(Risen, Draugs, Bone-callers)* | Undead (Skeletons, Ghouls, Necromancers) | The rot left behind when too many men die unburied where the Tide runs thick. **Risen** are bare bone-men who shrug off arrows; **Draugs** are fast, ravenous barrow-corpses; **Bone-callers** are the mad ex-priests, ruined witches, or bitter war-survivors who leash them. Kill the Bone-caller and the dead drop where they stand. |

## 6. Saints, Legends & Everyday Life

### Martyrs & Saints of the Vigil
Wayside shrines and saints' relics belong to real men and women of Roven's past *(replacing the placeholder Anglo-Saxon saints in `Campaign/Sites.cs`)*:

- **St. Bram the Lantern** — An abbey bell-ringer who kept the watch-fire lit for four nights while Draugs crawled up the hill. Patron of sentries and night-watches.
- **St. Mireille of the Ford** — A healer who stayed behind in a fever-struck town during the old border wars. Wounded companies still camp by her springs.
- **St. Odran the Mason** — Laid the first stone of Tarn's pass-wall and died with a trowel in one hand and a spear in the other. Patron of sappers and castle-builders.
- **St. Clovis the Unburnt** — A Vigil Lector who walked into a Bone-caller's barrow alone to break its ward. Soldiers carry his tokens against curses.
- **St. Ysabeau the Quiet** — An abbess who sheltered deserters from both sides until a duke hanged her at her own gate. Common levies swear by her.
- **St. Gerran of the Salt** — A fisherman who pulled forty shipwrecked sailors out of the Bay of Mists. Bayfolk and Brinemere crews nail his mark to the mast.
- **St. Halvar the Turned** — A Skar raider who dropped his axe at a wayside shrine and spent thirty years burying the war-dead. The only northern saint in the Vigil calendar.
- **St. Veren the Lame** — A crippled knight who held a bridge alone on a crutch so his baggage train could cross.
- **St. Alix of the Well** — Found the first clean spring after a three-year drought in the central hills.
- **St. Thibaut the Judge** — Hanged a baron's son for burning a village and was murdered on the steps of his own court.
- **St. Orla the Grey** — A midwife who walked across a battlefield at dusk to tend both sides.
- **St. Mael of the Pass** — Froze to death keeping the cairn-fires lit for pilgrims in a winter blizzard.

### Notable Figures & Living Legends
- **King Hugues IV of Brevon ("Hugues the Debtor"):** Mortgaged the lagoon ports to Brinemere's bankers seventy years ago to fund a disastrous winter invasion of Skar, then died of pneumonia before spring — leaving his heirs an empty treasury and a lost coast.
- **Temir Naryn ("the Iron Saddle"):** The steppe khan who broke twelve rival banners ninety years ago and forged the Naryn Khaganate. His grandson **Batu-Khar** married a Salt Sea port-heiress, founding the Salt-Riders.
- **Ser Roland of Castelnoir ("the Ash-Hand"):** The celebrated Knight-Commander who hit his Aura Wall forty years ago, entered the oldest highland barrows, and returned wearing the **Hollow Crown**. He burned three baronies before his own sworn brothers killed him in the snow.
- **Grand Master Odo of Tarn:** Marched two hundred grey cloaks between Brevon and Vespra at the **Ford of St. Mireille** during the Five-Year War to force a three-day burial truce so the dead would not rise. Still alive, blind in one eye, and feared by every ruler on Roven.
- **Lady Chiara of Vespra ("the Silver Rose"):** One of the very few living **Aura Masters** known by name — a Red Rose duellist who broke three Brevon lances in a single afternoon during the Five-Year War and now refuses every summons to court.
- **Hakim Al-Zahir of Qadir:** A century-old astronomer and physician whose star-tables and fever-treatises are copied in secret even in Brevon's abbeys — with his name scratched off the cover.

### Trade, Coin & Everyday Speech
- **Coin:** Soldiers call all silver coin **"crowns"** (after the old Brevon silver crown whose weight became the camp standard), though Brinemere's gold ducats, Qadir's dinars, and Skar's hacksilver rings all jingle in the same purses.
- **What Moves on the Roads & Sea Lanes:** Skar trades pine timber, furs, and salt herring; Brevon trades grain, wool, and heavy warhorses; Brinemere controls sea-salt, blown glass, and banking credit; Vespra trades fine steel, armour, and dyed cloth; Qadir trades spices, silk, dates, and star-charts; Naryn trades remounts, hides, and horn bows; Tarn trades nothing — it lives on pass-tolls, pious grants, and highland iron.
- **Time & Oaths:** Common folk count years from the last great war or a king's reign rather than one universal calendar. Everyday oaths mirror the faiths: *"By the Grey Light"*, *"St. Bram keep us"*, *"Under the Unblinking Sun"*, *"By the Wide Sky"*, *"May the Fells take him."*

## 7. Deeper Mysteries *(Deliberately Unresolved)*

- **The Thinning Tide:** Scholars in Brevon's abbeys and Qadir's observatories agree on one quiet fact: the Tide is weaker now than the oldest inscriptions describe. Whether the world is simply cooling like a dying hearth, or something deep under the Spine is drinking it, nobody knows.
- **The Barrow-Kings:** Old chronicles warn that after long wars, the Unquiet do not always stay in scattered grave fields. Once in a century, an older, crowned dead thing wakes in the deep highland barrows and calls the rest to it. Tarn keeps permanent watch-towers over the oldest barrow-fields for a reason.
